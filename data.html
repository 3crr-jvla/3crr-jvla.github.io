Planned products

For all 73 objects in the sample, at 0.3″ or 1″ resolution (objects at L-band without e-MERLIN data):

  • Final Stokes I maps, as compressed FITS images
  • De-rotated Stokes Q and U maps
  • Linearly polarised intensity maps
  • Spectral and Faraday depth cubes — available on request
  • The 0.3″ and 1″ LOFAR 144 MHz maps already produced by our team for 19 of the 73 objects

Using our calibration techniques we expect to reach theoretical noise levels of 10 µJy beam−1 at L-band and 5 µJy beam−1 at C-band, except for the brightest sources, which will be dynamic-range limited — making uv coverage, rather than deeper observations, the essential ingredient.

Processing

Our science goals and legacy value rely on thermal-noise (or dynamic-range) limited maps. The JVLA WIDAR correlator is well tested to produce dynamic ranges between 103 and 106 after polarisation and self-calibration. There is currently no dedicated, publicly available, fully automated polarisation and self-calibration pipeline for high dynamic range imaging of JVLA data.

We have developed polarisation and self-calibration pipelines dedicated to JVLA mapping at high dynamic range, and continue to develop both with the aim of fully automating the processing for all objects after they have been through the standard VLA calibration pipeline. We are also assessing the validity of baseline corrections (CASA BLCAL), which with sufficient uv coverage can substantially improve the dynamic range.

Raw data volume is expected to reach 10 TB, for which we have ample storage and compute processing nodes through the University of Hertfordshire High Performance Computing facility.
