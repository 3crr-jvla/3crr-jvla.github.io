Sample

73 3CRR radio galaxies at z < 1

Selected from the 172 entries of the 3CRR catalogue — the brightest radio galaxies in the northern sky, where multi-wavelength and particularly X-ray coverage is near-complete.

Selection

Redshift z < 1
Maximises value-added science for the community, where X-ray observations are near-complete for the sample.
Angular size between 20″ and 240″
The lower bound ensures at least 15 beams across each radio source (given the 1.2″ maximum resolution at L-band), allowing resolved spectral and polarimetric analysis. The upper bound keeps angular sizes within the largest angular scale of emission sensitive to the D-array at C-band, so integrated fluxes can be determined.
Missing JVLA coverage
55 of the 73 objects have no L- or C-band JVLA observations in any array configuration. Time is requested per source only where those data are absent from the archive or from our other projects — the outlined chips in the table below mark the configurations already covered.

Of the 172 catalogue entries, 37 lie at z ≥ 1, 26 exceed 240″, 35 are too compact to resolve, and one has no tabulated angular size — leaving the 73 targets of this programme. The resulting sample spans a wide range of morphologies and environments. More than 30 objects have X-ray jets and hotspots, and all are suspected to contain large-scale filaments.

Observing strategy

Per source and per band, and only where we lack these data in the archive or in our other projects, we observe 1 hour in the A-array, 30 minutes in B, 15 minutes in C and 5 minutes in D. Scheduling permitting, observations of different sources are interleaved to improve uv coverage.

That comes to 207 hours on source. Accounting for 40% overheads due to slew times and polarisation calibration, the total requested is 345 hours. Full polarimetric observations use the 8-bit samplers at L-band and the 3-bit samplers at C-band.
